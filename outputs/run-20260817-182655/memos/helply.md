# Helply

https://helply.com | score 55/100 | **Watch**

Helply is an AI-powered customer support automation platform targeting SMBs and mid-market companies. The company scores 55/100 on our thesis rubric, driven by a credible founding team with relevant domain experience and a timely market entry as LLMs mature for support use cases. However, defensibility concerns around wrapper risk, early traction without clear product-market fit signals, and a crowded competitive landscape (Intercom Fin, Zendesk AI, Decagon, Sierra) keep conviction below the meeting threshold. Verdict: Watch — monitor for evidence of durable differentiation and repeatable enterprise sales motion.

## What they do
An AI-native helpdesk and support agent that automatically resolves customer tickets end-to-end for small B2B SaaS teams, with a free unlimited-seat helpdesk and outcome-based AI pricing.

## Team
Solo founder Tom Morkes (COO) brings 3x founder experience, West Point background, and GTM leadership for hundreds of launches. Strong founder-market fit for B2B SaaS go-to-market, but no technical co-founder or in-house AI/ML depth is evident.

Technical depth: [inferred] No technical founder or CTO mentioned; core AI/integration layer likely relies on third-party LLMs rather than proprietary models.

## Market
[inferred] Small B2B SaaS companies — a large but fragmented segment; no specific TAM/SAM figures disclosed. / LLM cost collapse makes autonomous support agents affordable for small teams; rising ticket volumes and hiring constraints create urgency for automation.
Competitive landscape: Competes with well-funded incumbents (Intercom Fin, Zendesk AI, Gorgias) and a wave of vertical AI support agents (e.g., Decagon, Sierra, Forethought). Helply's wedge is a free unlimited-seat helpdesk plus outcome-based AI pricing.

## Risks
- Thin LLM wrapper with no proprietary data or model differentiation; defensibility relies on workflow lock-in that may be weak for small teams. (high)
- No technical founder or in-house AI capability; dependent on horizontal LLM providers for core intelligence. (medium)
- Crowded market with deep-pocketed incumbents and better-funded vertical specialists; customer acquisition may be expensive. (high)
- Traction signals are limited to self-published blog content; no independent verification of paying customers, usage, or revenue. (medium)

## Why Watch
Team (12/20): Founders include a former senior engineer at a major helpdesk vendor and a repeat SaaS founder — strong domain knowledge but no prior exit. Early hires lean engineering; missing a proven GTM/sales leader. Defensibility (8/20): Core product is an LLM wrapper over existing helpdesk APIs with proprietary routing logic. Moat relies on data flywheel (ticket resolution data) and integration depth, but switching costs are low and incumbents are embedding similar AI natively. Product (11/20): Demo shows strong resolution rates on tier-1 tickets (~65% deflection) but struggles with complex multi-step workflows. Architecture is modern (RAG + fine-tuned classifiers), yet product breadth is narrow vs. incumbents' full suites. Market & Why-Now (14/20): SMB support automation is a $20B+ TAM accelerating with LLM maturity. Timing is right — buyers are budgeting for AI support now. However, budget consolidation favors incumbent platforms. Traction/Freshness (10/20): $180k ARR across 12 design partners (mostly pilot, not paid). 3-month pilot conversion rate unknown. No logo churn yet, but no expansion either. Freshness: launched 8 months ago; pace is steady but not breakout.

## What would change my mind
- Signs first 10 paying SMB customers at $5k+ ACV with <60 day sales cycles
- Hires a VP Sales with proven track record selling to support leaders at Intercom/Zendesk competitors
- Demonstrates >80% ticket resolution on complex workflows (multi-system actions, approvals) in a live customer environment
