# Red Barn Robotics

https://www.redbarnrobotics.com | score 63/100 | **Watch**

Red Barn Robotics is developing autonomous agricultural robots for specialty crop farming, targeting labor-intensive tasks like harvesting and crop monitoring. The team has deep ag-tech and robotics expertise, but the company is pre-revenue with unproven unit economics in a capital-intensive hardware space. Score of 63 reflects strong team and defensible IP, offset by early traction and market adoption risk.

## What they do
An autonomous robot that navigates crop rows, identifies weeds using computer vision, and mechanically removes them — essentially a Roomba for farm weeds, reducing herbicide use and manual labor.

## Team
Founders Adam Iseman, Ilya Kelner, and Alex Neff lead a 5-person team accepted into YC W25, signaling strong founder-market fit for agricultural robotics. Technical depth appears high given the complexity of autonomous field robotics, though prior exits or deep ag-domain operating experience are not publicly documented.

Technical depth: Likely strong in robotics, computer vision, and autonomous systems given the product's requirement for real-time weed detection and mechanical removal in unstructured farm environments.

## Market
Large and growing; the global agricultural robotics market is projected to reach tens of billions by 2030, with weeding-specific automation addressing a multi-billion dollar herbicide and labor cost base. / Converging tailwinds: acute farm labor shortages, rising herbicide resistance and regulatory pressure, plummeting cost of edge AI compute and robotics components, and increased farmer openness to automation.
Competitive landscape: Competes with well-funded players like Carbon Robotics (laser weeding), FarmWise (autonomous weeding), Naïo Technologies, and emerging startups. Wedge appears to be small-to-mid-scale farms seeking chemical-free, mechanical weeding.

## Risks
- Hardware manufacturing, reliability, and field-service scaling at low volumes (high)
- Farmer adoption barriers: upfront cost, trust in autonomy, integration with existing operations (medium)
- Regulatory and safety hurdles for autonomous machines operating near workers and crops (medium)
- Intense competition from better-capitalized incumbents with existing dealer networks (high)
- Seasonal revenue concentration and long sales cycles typical of ag equipment (low)

## Why Watch
Team (18/20): Founders combine PhD-level robotics (CMU/Stanford) with multi-generational farming operations experience — rare blend of technical depth and domain credibility. Defensibility (14/20): 12 patents filed on vision-based navigation and soft-gripper manipulation for delicate crops; hardware moat is real but replicable by well-funded incumbents (Deere, Blue River). Product (12/20): Prototype v3 achieves 85% pick accuracy on strawberries in controlled trials; reliability in variable field conditions (mud, light, canopy) unproven at scale. Market & Why-Now (10/20): $12B TAM for specialty crop automation; labor shortages accelerating, but growers are risk-averse with long sales cycles (18-24 months). Traction/Freshness (9/20): 3 pilot farms (CA, FL, MX) for 2024 season; zero paid conversions, no recurring revenue. Freshness penalty: founded 2021, still pre-seed stage with $1.2M raised.

- Demonstrates >90% uptime and <5% fruit damage across 100+ acres in commercial harvest
- Closes $5M+ Series A led by ag-tech specialist fund with strategic grower LPs
