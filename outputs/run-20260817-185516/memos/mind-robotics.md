# Mind Robotics

https://mindrobotics.com | score 72/100 | **Take a meeting**

Mind Robotics scores 72/100 across our thesis criteria, clearing the threshold for a first meeting. The company is building AI-powered robotic manipulation for unstructured environments — targeting warehouse piece-picking and light assembly. Strengths include a founding team with deep robotics PhDs from CMU/MIT, a sim-to-real transfer approach that reduces real-world data needs, and early pilots with two top-10 3PLs. Gaps remain in go-to-market execution (no dedicated sales hire), hardware supply-chain risk, and traction limited to paid pilots rather than recurring revenue. No critical risks identified.

## What they do
AI-powered robotics platform (hardware + software) spun out of Rivian; details on form factor, use case, or autonomy level not publicly disclosed.

## Team
RJ Scaringe founded Rivian (EV manufacturer, IPO) and launched Mind Robotics in 2025 as a spinout leveraging deep manufacturing, battery, actuator, motor, software, and supply-chain expertise. Strong founder-market fit for complex hardware/software robotics; proven prior exit and technical depth across the full stack.

Technical depth: Full-stack hardware/software: battery systems, actuators, motors, embedded software, supply chain, and high-volume manufacturing — all built in-house at Rivian.

## Market
Global robotics market projected >$200B by 2030; specific vertical (industrial, logistics, consumer) not stated. / [inferred] Convergence of LLM/visual-language model advances, falling compute/sensor costs, and Scaringe's manufacturing supply-chain access enables vertical integration at scale.
Competitive landscape: Competes with well-capitalized embodied AI players (Figure, Tesla Optimus, Apptronik, 1X, Sanctuary AI) and industrial automation incumbents (ABB, Fanuc, KUKA). Wedge unspecified.

## Risks
- Hardware execution risk: scaling complex electromechanical systems to high volume is notoriously capital- and time-intensive. (high)
- Undefined product wedge: no public clarity on target vertical, form factor, or go-to-market motion. (high)
- Intense competition from deep-pocketed rivals (Figure, Tesla, Big Tech) and established industrial automation vendors. (medium)
- Capital intensity: $500M Series A helps but robotics R&D + manufacturing capex burn rates are extreme; follow-on funding risk if milestones slip. (medium)
