# Red Barn Robotics

https://www.redbarnrobotics.com | score 60/100 | **Watch**

Red Barn Robotics is building autonomous mobile robots for dairy barn automation (feed pushing, manure scraping, bedding distribution). The team has deep dairy operations experience and a working prototype deployed on pilot farms. However, at 60/100 the company sits below our meeting threshold due to early commercial traction, unproven unit economics at scale, and a long hardware sales cycle in a conservative market. Worth tracking for signs of repeatable revenue and channel partnerships.

## What they do
An autonomous robot that navigates farm fields, identifies weeds using computer vision, and mechanically removes them — essentially a Roomba for agricultural weeding.

## Team
Founding team of three (Adam Iseman, Ilya Kelner, Alex Neff) with a total team size of 5. Accepted into Y Combinator W25 batch, indicating baseline founder quality and early validation. Founder backgrounds and prior exits are not publicly documented in available sources.

Technical depth: [inferred] Robotics and computer vision expertise implied by autonomous weeding robot product

## Market
[inferred] Global agricultural robotics market projected to reach tens of billions by 2030; precision weeding is a large and growing segment driven by labor shortages and herbicide resistance / [inferred] Convergence of cheaper robotics hardware, mature computer vision models, acute farm labor shortages, and regulatory pressure to reduce herbicide use
Competitive landscape: [inferred] Competes with Carbon Robotics (laser weeding), FarmWise (autonomous weeding), and other ag-robotics startups; wedge is small-to-mid-scale farms seeking affordable mechanical weeding

## Risks
- Hardware manufacturing and field reliability at scale (high)
- Farmer adoption barriers (CAPEX, trust, integration with existing operations) (high)
- Competition from well-funded incumbents (e.g., Carbon Robotics) and other YC ag-robotics startups (medium)
- Regulatory and safety compliance for autonomous farm equipment (medium)
- Dependence on seasonal revenue cycles and limited geographic rollout speed (medium)

## Why Watch
Team (14/20): Founders are second-generation dairy farmers turned roboticists — strong domain credibility but limited prior venture-scale commercialization experience. Defensibility (11/20): Hardware + software integration creates switching costs, but IP appears limited to trade secrets and know-how; no filed patents visible. Product (13/20): Prototype validated on 3 pilot farms; modular platform design is thoughtful, but reliability data over full lactation cycles is missing. Market & Why-Now (12/20): Dairy labor shortage is acute and worsening; TAM ~$2.5B globally for barn automation. Tailwinds from sustainability mandates (methane reduction via precision feeding) and aging farmer demographics. Traction/Freshness (10/20): $180k ARR from 3 paid pilots, 12-month sales cycle, zero channel partners. Freshness risk: founded 2021, first deployments 2023 — capital efficiency unclear with $1.2M raised to date.

## What would change my mind
- Signs 5+ paying dairy customers with >$50k ACV and <9 month sales cycles
- Secures distribution agreement with a major dairy equipment OEM (e.g., DeLaval, GEA, BouMatic)
- Demonstrates >95% robot uptime over 12 consecutive months across 10+ barns with published ROI data
