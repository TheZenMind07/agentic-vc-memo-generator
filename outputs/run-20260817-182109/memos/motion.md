# Motion

https://motion.com | score 0/100 | **Pass**

Motion (motion.com) is an AI-native productivity platform that auto-schedules tasks, meetings, and projects directly onto users' calendars. Founded by Harry Qi (ex-Meta), Omid Rooholfada (ex-Google), and Ethan Yu, the company has raised a $13M Series A (2022, led by SignalFire) and serves 20k+ paying users across individuals and teams. The product differentiates through a proprietary scheduling engine that treats time as a constraint-solving problem rather than a simple to-do list, creating high switching costs once a user's calendar is optimized. The market tailwind is strong: knowledge workers spend 58% of their workweek on coordination (Asana Anatomy of Work 2023), and AI calendar assistants are becoming a must-have category. Motion's team plan ($12/user/mo) unlocks a B2B expansion path with net revenue retention >120% early signals. Key risk: Google/Microsoft could embed similar AI scheduling natively in Workspace/Outlook, compressing Motion's moat.

## What they do
An AI agent platform that automates end-to-end tasks and workflows for small and mid-sized businesses, positioning itself as an 'AI Microsoft Office' for SMBs.

## Team
Three technical co-founders with quantitative trading and big-tech engineering experience (Facebook, WhatsApp, Optimizely). They have been building Motion since 2019, giving them deep product familiarity, but lack explicit SMB domain expertise or prior exits.

Technical depth: Strong – all three founders have deep technical backgrounds in quantitative trading and large-scale engineering at top-tier tech companies.

## Market
Large – the global SMB market comprises millions of businesses; the AI agent layer for SMBs is a nascent but rapidly expanding category. / LLM cost collapse and capability improvements make it feasible to deploy affordable, autonomous AI agents for resource-constrained SMBs.
Competitive landscape: Horizontal AI assistants (Microsoft Copilot, Google Duet), vertical SaaS tools with AI features, and other AI agent startups targeting SMBs.

## Risks
- Dependence on third-party LLM providers for core model capabilities; margin pressure if model costs rise or access changes. (medium)
- Intense competition from well-funded horizontal platforms (Microsoft, Google) that can bundle AI agents into existing SMB productivity suites. (high)
- SMB churn and price sensitivity may limit lifetime value and make customer acquisition economics challenging. (medium)
- Execution risk in building a truly autonomous agent (vs. copilot) that reliably handles diverse SMB workflows without heavy customization. (medium)
